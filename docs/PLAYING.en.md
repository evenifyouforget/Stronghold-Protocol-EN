# Gameplay guide

> English translation of [PLAYING.md](PLAYING.md). The Chinese original is the authoritative version. Game terms follow the official EN client (as used by the in-game English UI, `public/i18n/en.json`).

*Stronghold Protocol: Alliance* is auto-chess + tower defense: during the Rest Phase you recruit Operators at the Dispatch Center, arrange your formation and assign Equipment; during the Battle Phase your Operators deploy automatically and hold off the enemies pouring out of the red gates, and every enemy that gets through costs you Life Points (LP). In Team Simulation, 1–4 players **cooperate** (there is no PvP); survive to round 14 and defeat the Enemy Leader to win.

The numbers in this document come from the official data tables (`data/config.json`, etc.) and the [research](research/00-INDEX.md); in game, 📖 "How to Play" in the bottom-left corner has the official tutorial images (downloaded with the assets; if they weren't downloaded, bullet-point text is shown instead). For controls, see [README · Controls](../README.en.md#controls).

## 1. Flow of a match

```
Confirm Match Info (Team: 25 s) → Choose Strategy (draft) → Execute Protocol
→ each round: [Improv] → Rest Phase → Battle → [Unite] → Settlement
→ round 14: Final Assault → (if conditions are met) round 15: Hidden Core → result titles
```

| Phase | Description |
|---|---|
| Confirm Match Info | The match's map (Standard Simulation is always "Battlefield #01"; Perilous picks randomly from 8 maps, Dire / Ultimate randomly from the 7 that exclude Battlefield #01), the enemy faction, **the disabled Alliances and their Operators**, and the Leader preview. Continues once everyone has confirmed; at most 25 seconds in Team Simulation; untimed when you are the only player (Solo Simulation, or Team Simulation with only AI teammates). |
| Choose Strategy | The 40 Strategies (squad leaders) determine your **starting LP (20–45)** and one permanent rule. Team: players pick in turn in a random order, **30 seconds each**; the top right and "Deciding" show the same countdown; AI teammates pick instantly; a Strategy a teammate has taken can't be picked again; each player can **skip once** (moving to the end of the order); on timeout your **currently selected** Strategy is picked automatically (Warfarin if a teammate already took it). Solo: free choice, untimed; Team Simulation with you as the only player is also untimed. While choosing you can click "View disabled Alliances & Operators" under the pick order on the left to review the Alliances from "Confirm Match Info" (greyed out, number disabled) and the Operators disabled this match: view-only — the countdown keeps running, and the bottom shows whose turn it is and the seconds left; close it with "Close", by clicking outside the window or with Esc; it closes automatically when it's the next player's turn. |
| Improv | Appears before some rounds: in Team, 6 cards in total and players take 1 each in turn (30 seconds for the first, 16 for the rest; untimed when you are the only player); in Solo, pick 1 of 3. Cards include Equipment, Funds, Operators, Alliance Stacks, Bounties, etc. **Bounty Decision** adds Bounty enemies to your battles, with the duration marked in blue on the card: "next battle" / "next two battles" bounties appear in one or two battles only and then no more; the official "multi-round bounties" (original text: "every battle after this") are also treated as "next two battles" here and appear in only two battles (e.g. Pointing Stick's Arts Master A2 · multi-round tactical training). The Bounty Decisions of each round follow official match screenshots: round 3 offers 6 "next two battles" bounties (3 costing 1 Fund, 2 costing 2 Funds, 1 costing 3 Funds); round 9 offers each boss's "next battle" bounty and "Originium Slug/Training"; round 11 on Dire / Ultimate is usually a Bounty Decision (it may also be a Secret Shop or Tactical Decision), whose bounties usually include 1 special giant beast, the rest being "next battle" bounties from each faction. Whoever knocks out a Bounty enemy (teammates during Unite count too) gets the Funds; the effects bar on the right shows "N battles left". All 6 pieces of Equipment in the **Secret Shop** are free, and, as in the official game, two identical pieces may appear (each counts as a separate card and can be taken separately); the round-11 (Dire / Ultimate) Secret Shop follows the official game: 2 tier-VI pieces, at least 1 tier-V piece and 1 Alliance Coin, while in earlier Secret Shops on Standard / Perilous any tier may appear. **Tactical Decision** can also offer two identical cards as in the official game (two "Supply" cards have been seen in official screenshots; each counts as a separate card and can be taken separately, each takes effect only once, and if two players each pick a team-wide card the effects stack); the round-11 (Dire / Ultimate) Tactical Decision, per official screenshots, only offers buff cards such as Vow, Reinforce, Equip / Wealth / Supply / Prepare / Promotion, and no enemy-weakening or battlefield-evolution cards. Each card shows its icon and name at the top left with the full effect below (the same official description as the detail card, also shown on phones); cards already taken are dimmed, with the avatar of the player who took it in the top-right corner (hover to see the name). Picking a card takes **two taps**: the first only selects it (gold frame + "Confirm · Tap again"); tapping the same card again or the "Confirm" at the top right makes it take effect; tap another card to switch, or press Esc / tap empty space to cancel. |
| Rest Phase | Buy, arrange your formation, assign Equipment, Freeze / Refresh, upgrade the Dispatch Center; Team has a countdown (65–215 seconds, increasing with the round); Solo Simulation and Team Simulation where you are the only player (with only AI teammates) are untimed. When everyone is "Ready" the battle starts early; you can cancel before everyone is ready. |
| Battle | Each player fights on their own battlefield (the battle is simulated in your browser, at 2× speed). Operators deploy automatically in **top-to-bottom, left-to-right** order and spend DP (starting at 10, +1/sec). The time limit is 45–115 seconds (real seconds, i.e. the countdown on screen); enemies still alive when time runs out count as leaks. When an enemy enters the blue gate, the LP in the top bar **immediately** shows the value after the deduction (red text + "−N", at most 10 per round), finalized at Settlement; during the Unite phase it is marked "Uniting", and the part teammates intercept is not deducted in the end. The team list on the left shows teammates' pending LP loss the same way. A knocked-out Operator **stays on the tile where it fell** (lying down, slightly greyed) and redeploys on that same tile later (as in the official game; one that falls on another Operator's or a summon's starting position goes back to lie down at its own starting position, even if the summon has disappeared); Operators forced off the field by their own effects (the end of Surtr's "Remnant Ash", the end of Nearl the Radiant Knight's S2, etc.) likewise fall in place and redeploy in place; the tile of a downed Operator **is not taken by anything else** — Raid Alliance redeploys, reinforcements and summons all avoid it. The circle over its head is the redeploy countdown: a green ring + seconds; it turns into a yellow "DP" when there isn't enough DP. The row under the HP bar is **Elemental Damage** (as in the original: element icon + white bar): Operators get a colored disc with an icon and enemies a small solid disc; the white bar on the right is the remaining element value and decreases right-to-left like an HP bar, and the moment it runs out is the burst (when a large chunk of Elemental Damage lands at once the white bar drops by a chunk too — e.g. each hit of a Skimming Sea Drifter is about 25%–50%); after bursting comes the burst cooldown (Burn / Nervous Impairment / Corrosion 10 seconds, enemy Corrosion 8 seconds, Necrosis 15 seconds), during which the bar turns the **element's color** (Nervous Impairment teal, Corrosion blue, Burn orange-red, Necrosis grey-purple), slowly refills over the cooldown and the icon flashes — a colored bar means the unit has already burst and is under the burst effect, not the element value remaining; during this time no element accumulates and none can be healed, and it resets to zero afterwards. Elemental bursts deal **source-less damage** that doesn't benefit from the Operator's damage buffs, penetration or Equipment bonuses (e.g. Underflow's Burn burst against Seaborn is a fixed 7000), but the kill still goes to the Operator who triggered the burst; Elemental Fragility only increases elemental damage and doesn't speed up the element bar; Fragile doesn't affect elemental damage. Enemies already killed can't receive further Elemental Damage. |
| Unite | See section 5. |
| Settlement | Deduct LP, pay out Bounty Funds, settle Stacks; players with LP ≤ 0 are eliminated (their Operators go back to the shared pool). |

**Disconnecting and leaving**: in Team Simulation, reopen the page within 10 minutes of a disconnect to return to your seat; meanwhile your formation fights automatically as usual, and Improv and the Rest Phase pick / ready up automatically when time runs out, but nothing is bought for you (to have the AI play for you, choose "Step Away (AI Autopilot)" under "Leave Simulation"; AI Autopilot won't destroy your items to make room — when the Bench is full it sells benched Operators to make space for items). In Solo Simulation the Rest Phase and Improv are untimed; after a disconnect or closing the page, open it in the same browser **within 24 hours** to continue; Solo Simulation battles (including Final Assault / Hidden Core) can also be paused at any time with "Pause" in the top bar (or Space), which stops the countdown and the Leader-fight overtime LP drain together (if you disconnect while paused it resumes automatically and the server finishes the battle). Team Simulation battles cannot be paused. "Abandon Simulation": in Solo Simulation it ends the match immediately; in Team Simulation it counts as you being eliminated (your Operators go back to the shared pool) and your teammates carry on. Restarting the server ends every match.

**Rounds and Improv rounds**

| Difficulty | Team: rounds / Improv rounds | Solo: rounds / Improv rounds |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Simulation | 14 / 3, 9 | 9 (round 9 is the Leader) / none |
| Perilous Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 6, 9 | 14 (+15) / 6, 9 |
| Dire Simulation · Ultimate Simulation | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 | 14 (+15) / 3, 9, 11 |

The higher the difficulty, the higher the enemies' ATK / HP multipliers, the more dangerous enemies appear, and the more HP the Leader has.

| Leftover Funds | **Reset to zero at the end of the Rest Phase** (except with the Strategy "Cannot") |
| Recruit price (by tier I–VI) | 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 4 / 4 |

Equipment can't be sold; on the Bench it can only be "Destroyed" (no refund). The Dispatch Center level determines the **highest tier** that can appear in the shop and the number of slots (Perilous and above: 3 / 4 / 4 / 5 / 5 / 5 Operator slots + 1 Equipment slot).

## 3. Recruiting, the Bench and promotion

- **Buying takes two taps**: the first tap on a shop card only selects it (the card lifts and opens its details, and "Confirm Purchase · Tap again" appears at the bottom); tapping **anywhere** on the card again (including the gold confirm bar) buys it; tap elsewhere or press Esc to cancel. Upgrading the Dispatch Center and Promotion Rewards also take two taps; free picks from Strategies or Equipment (such as the 3 pieces of Equipment refreshed after Catherine's 【Targeted Drop】 upgrade, the 2 pieces of Equipment from Narantuya's 【Passing Down the Profit】, or the 3 Operators from the Dispatch Module) also appear in the shop card slots, with their source written in the title on the left; Equipment is shown as Equipment cards (icon, name, description, FREE); pick 1 to get it for free. When several free picks are queued they appear one after another, and the title and the hint button after "Later" say how many remain (e.g. "+1"). When you can't buy (e.g. you're already Ready), tapping a card only opens its details.
- **Shared pool** (Team): each Operator has a limited number of copies — 12 / 14 / 18 / 16 / 8 / 5 for tiers I–VI — shared by all players; Operators you hold (board + Bench) use up copies, while those displayed in the shop don't. Once everyone has taken all copies of an Operator it can't be rolled any more; eliminated players' Operators return to the pool.
- **Slot limits**: at most **8** deployed on the board; **10** Bench slots; when the Bench is full, newly obtained units (merged Elites, rewards, returned Equipment, etc.) go to the **Temporary Bench** (the 5 slots in front of the Bench). While the Temporary Bench holds units you **can't Ready**; the board marks that row with a red dashed frame and explains the rule: move them to the Bench or battlefield, assign or use them, or sell / destroy them. Units that enter the Temporary Bench during a Rest Phase are handled automatically if still there at **the end of that Rest Phase** (Operators return to the pool, Equipment is destroyed); units that enter during battle, Settlement or after you're Ready (e.g. battle rewards, Elites merged at Settlement) are kept until the end of the **next Rest Phase**, so you always get a chance to deal with them.
- **Elite promotion**: 3 copies of the same Operator (deployed or not) automatically merge into an **Elite** (big boosts to level, skills and Module; Kazemaru needs only 2), and Elites don't merge further. If any of the merged Operators is **already deployed on the battlefield**, the Elite appears directly **in that Operator's position** (keeping the original facing and not taking an extra deployment slot; if two or three are on the field, the one deployed first — top-to-bottom, left-to-right — is used); otherwise the Elite goes to the Bench (the Temporary Bench if full). All previously assigned Equipment returns to the Bench and must be reassigned; the merged Operators' summons are removed, and an Elite on the field sends its own summons to the Bench. When you select a shop card that would complete a merge, the tile where the Elite will appear lights up with a gold border, and the detail card that opens alongside states where it goes ("Promotable: the Elite Operator will appear at the original position in the battle area / will go to the Bench"; the detail card docks automatically on the side that doesn't cover that tile). Merges completed at round start or Settlement through Strategies, rewards, etc. also play the gold promotion effect when the Elite appears.
- **Promotion Reward**: each promotion grants one Special Recruit: pick 1 **for free** from 3 **distinct** random Operators of the **next tier up** (max VI) (if fewer than 3 Operators remain at that tier, the rest are filled from the tier below). It can't be Refreshed or Frozen, and expires at the end of the round.
- Each round the shop automatically refreshes the slots that aren't frozen.
- **"First manual Refresh this round" is counted per Operator** (Lappland): it applies to the first manual Refresh she encounters this round, even on the Bench — Refresh first, then buy Lappland, and the next Refresh still stacks for her; an Elite merged during the same round from Lapplands whose effect already triggered doesn't trigger again this round.

## 4. Formation and facing

- Drag Operators from the Bench onto the board: melee / ranged tiles are highlighted as valid spots. The trait of **Hookmasters and Push Strokers** says "can be deployed on ranged tiles", so they can go on both ground and ranged tiles (in this season's pool only Gladiia is available; Cliffheart and Enforcer are hidden Operators); on a ranged tile they attack as usual but don't block enemies. **No deploying in water**: the pool of water down the middle of Battlefield #08 (Tide Controller) (Deep Water) refuses deployment just like the official game, so no Operator or summon can be placed there; Raid Alliance redeploys and Tacticians' reinforcements auto-picking a tactical point also only land on shore. After you release, the **direction wheel** appears: swipe up / right / down / left, the board previews the rotated attack range with orange stripes, and release to confirm; release in the center or tap "✕ Tap to cancel" to cancel.
- To change only the facing: drag the Operator back onto its own tile and choose a direction. Facing is kept into the next round.
- **Selection works by board tile**: tapping a tile selects the unit standing on that tile, and tapping an empty tile selects nothing (tapping the head of the Operator drawn on the tile behind counts as tapping that tile behind). During battle, allies are also selected by tile; enemies move between tiles, so tap their body or near their feet (within 0.6 tiles); for flying enemies, tap their body.
- While dragging, the model stays under your pointer / finger (feet about half a tile below the pointer, the same for mouse and touch), and **the tile under the pointer** is where it lands and is highlighted; Equipment dragged to a tile is assigned to the Operator on that tile; releasing over the shop bar puts it back where it was.
- Tapping an Operator shows its attack range and the bottom buttons ("Retreat" back to the Bench, "Sell +1"); right-click / long-press to view details: next to the portrait are its Alliances, and the first item below is the Operator's **Attribute** (e.g. "At the end of the Rest Phase …"), followed by trait, stats, attack range, skills, Module, Equipment and talents. Stats are **live values**: during battle they update 4 times per second (current HP, ATK, DEF, RES, Attack Interval, Block); during the Rest Phase they show the board Operator's values **at battle start** (with Equipment, Alliance Stacks, Attributes, Strategy and Improv effects included); green when above base and red when below, with the difference shown (a shorter Attack Interval counts as a buff); hover to see the base value.
- **Summons are placed by hand**: after an Operator with deployable summons is put on the board, its summons go to the Bench (Silence's Medic Drone, Shamare's Cursed Doll, Catherine's 2 support devices, Skadi the Corrupting Heart's Seaborn, Vigil's Wolfpack, Muelsyse's Flowing Shapes; summons of the same Operator stack in one slot and don't take a deployment slot), and are dragged onto the board and given a facing like Operators. Catherine's support device gives a Barrier to the Operator on **the tile it faces**, so place it next to an Operator, facing it. **Tacticians' reinforcements** (Vigil's Wolfpack, Muelsyse's Flowing Shapes — i.e. picking the tactical point) can, as in the official game, **only be placed within the summoner's attack range**: only those tiles light up while dragging; if you later change the Tactician's facing in place, reinforcements outside the new attack range return to the Bench (those still in range stay put; if both the Bench and Temporary Bench are full and the reinforcements have nowhere to go, the facing change doesn't apply and "Bench is full" is shown). Retreating or selling an Operator removes its summons with it; **moving an Operator to another tile (including dragging a summon onto the Operator to swap places) returns its placed summons to the Bench**, and they must be placed again. When the Bench is full, returned summons go to the Temporary Bench, and any not handled by the end of the Rest Phase return to the Bench next round.
- In battle: Seaborn, Wolfpack, Flowing Shapes and support devices deploy at their placed positions at battle start; the Medic Drone and Cursed Doll are also deployed once for free at their placed positions at battle start (lasting 10 / 15 seconds; as in the official game no skill activation is needed first), and afterwards reappear at the same spot each time their Operator activates its skill; when the owning Operator is knocked out and redeploys, if the one granted by the skill hasn't appeared yet, it appears immediately. When Shamare leaves the field the Cursed Doll retreats with her; when Silence leaves the field, a Medic Drone already out is unaffected. Medic Drones, Cursed Dolls, support devices and Seaborn don't appear unless placed; Wolfpack and Flowing Shapes (Tacticians' reinforcements) appear at the tactical point if not placed.
- **Blocking is by contact**: an Operator blocks enemies within about 0.7 tiles of its center that are in contact with it (ground Operators can't block flying or low-altitude hovering enemies). When an enemy is wedged between two Operators and the one blocking it falls, retreats or is stunned, another Operator still in contact with it with spare Block **immediately takes over blocking**; the enemy only walks off when nobody has a free slot. Enemies walking head-on stop in front of the Operator's tile. **If it's blocked, it can be hit**: any Operator (including ranged Operators and summons on melee tiles) can always attack the enemies it blocks, whether or not they're in its attack range and whichever side they're on, and attacks them first (except Medics: Medics other than Incantation Medics keep healing teammates while blocking and don't attack the blocked enemy; also except during "stop attacking" skills; skills whose original text has other restrictions follow that text: during Provence's S2 she doesn't target enemies above 80% HP, and the icicles of Santalla's S2 land on random tiles within her attack range).
- At battle start Operators deploy in **top-to-bottom, left-to-right** order, with summons after all Operators. Blocked enemies hit the Operator blocking them first; unblocked ranged enemies hit the highest Taunt level first, and among equals the most recently deployed (summons, Operators lower and further right, and freshly redeployed Operators get hit more easily). Invisible Operators are only attacked by enemies they block; splash, explosions, area skills and lingering damage zones from enemies they aren't blocking (including the toxic mist an OpFor: Erosion sprays when knocked out), and the attack-speed-reducing auras of the Dublinn Companion Guard and Zaro also don't land on them (as in the official game; effects that explicitly "ignore untargetable", such as Contamination Corrosion, still hit, and Frost's attack-speed-reducing aura and map effects like 'Gravestone' still apply); **Camouflage** (such as after Vulpisfoglia knocks out an enemy during her S3, or Kroos the Keen Glint's S1) is likewise only attacked by the enemies it blocks and is also shown semi-transparent, but does not avoid splash, explosions or area damage (normal attacks that hit every unit in an area at once — like 'Armor Cleaver', 'Armor Crusher' and Sudaram — can't hit it) and doesn't count as Invisible (it doesn't trigger Siracusa and other "while Invisible" effects). Siracusa's Invisible ends on a timer (32 seconds + 0.4 seconds per Stack); Vulpisfoglia's Camouflage, per the official game, lasts until her next skill activation. **While Tippi is airborne** she only blocks flying enemies and ground enemies don't target her (normal attacks, splash, skill areas, burning zones and debuffs that need a target don't land on her); flying and hovering enemies, and effects that explicitly "ignore untargetable" (such as Contamination Corrosion), still hit her; ground enemies' auras and field-wide effects (such as 'Gravestone' halving ATK and DEF across the field) and counters triggered when she attacks enemies (such as the Heavy Scout's exposure) still apply, and damage over time applied before take-off keeps ticking.
- **Skills fire automatically** according to the official "skill strategy": typical manual skills fire when ready, about to attack / heal, and with an enemy in the initial attack range (healing skills: an injured teammate); skills **with their own skill range** (such as Texas's "Sword Rain", SilverAsh the Reignfrost's "Countergambit", Degenbrecher's "Return To Silence", excluding "Attack Range expands"-type skills) fire as soon as **an enemy appears in the skill range**, without attacking first; **Defenders'** manual skills fire on taking damage (exceptions: Underflow's and Liskarm's S2, Horn's S2 and S3, and Ashlock's S1 and S2 were changed per player feedback to fire whenever an enemy is in attack range, and Yu's S2 "For Guests, Bounteous Gifts" was changed to fire whenever an enemy is within two tiles around him (the skill range) — this game intentionally differs from the official one here; Underflow's S1 still requires taking damage); Standard Bearers / Tacticians / Bards fire as soon as the skill is ready, and Liberators / Phalanx Casters fire when an enemy is in the initial attack range. **Auto-trigger** skills follow their own rules: e.g. Gummy's "Provisions" fires when an injured teammate is nearby (within skill range), turning her next attack into a heal on that teammate (no need to engage first; her S2 "Cooking" is a manual skill and fires on taking damage per the Defender rule); Saria's "First Aid" only fires when she's about to attack and a teammate at or below half HP is nearby, and that attack becomes a heal instead; the shield from Exusiai the New Covenant's S2, per the official game, goes to the teammate with the **highest aggro** in attack range (higher Taunt level first, and among equals the most recently deployed), which isn't necessarily the one at the front taking hits. Automatic firing of manual skills has a 3-second cooldown: after deploying at battle start it first waits 3 seconds, and afterwards the same Operator's automatic activations are at least 3 seconds apart (skills with charges won't dump all charges at once).
- **Push / pull** distance is set by the official "force − weight": e.g. Wild Mane's S2 (medium force) pushes enemies of weight 0 / 1 / 2 / 3 about 2.1 / 1.7 / 0.4 / 0.1 tiles, and can't move weight 4 or above — lighter enemies get pushed further. Wild Mane's S2 pushes along her facing: within 45° straight ahead the distances above apply, while enemies behind, beside or right against her only get a small nudge (within about 0.4 tiles); the coins of Swire the Elegant Wit's S3 instead push outward from her and land on ground enemies within 2–4 tiles in front of her (official "range 2-4") and on the enemies she blocks. Attack speed bottoms out at 20 (it won't get slower even with fully stacked slows from swamps, Frost, etc.).
- The **Elemental Damage bar** sits right under the unit's own HP bar: the element icon on the left, and the white bar is the remaining element value, which only runs out on an actual burst; after bursting the bar turns the element's color and the icon flashes, slowly refilling over the cooldown (no Elemental Damage accumulates during that time).
- **Enemies**: per the official game, ranged enemies' range is measured from the Operator's collision body (radius 0.25 tiles), reaching 0.25 tiles further than the attack-range radius in the data — e.g. the Sarkaz Wither Tank (range 2.2) can hit Operators on ground tiles within 2.45 tiles of its center. Unblocked ranged enemies stop for each attack and finish the attack animation, only moving on between attacks. Its first attack is always a "corrosion bombardment" (leaving a 1.7-radius contamination zone at the target for 10 seconds: 50 true damage per second on ground tiles, 25 on ranged tiles, same-name zones don't stack), and its normal attack damage doubles while blocked. Enemies such as 'Wings of the Sankta' fly around randomly in a small area in place for 5 seconds the first time their HP drops below half (officially called "Fear"); Fear inflicted by Operators such as Siracusa and Nymph makes enemies flee away from the Operator. Invisible enemies can only be targeted while blocked (or revealed), and splash and circular skill-area damage can't hit unblocked invisible enemies (as in the official game); after a block ends they can still be targeted for 3 seconds (enemies whose official page says 0 or 1 second follow the page) before turning invisible again: each time a Dublinn Flamechaser Soldier is knocked out it first takes 1 second to rebirth, then becomes an invisible "Remnant Ash" that keeps walking (it takes 5 hits to knock out, 10 for the escort's ash), which usually has to be blocked by an Operator to be targeted (it stops being semi-transparent while blocked; if it was knocked out while blocked, it can also be finished off with ranged or area damage for about 2 seconds after the rebirth ends), and stands back up at full HP if not cleared within 10 seconds; on rebirth an enemy's slow, fear and other statuses are cleared and its skill cooldowns start over, and a feared Flamechaser that turns to ash can still be blocked; Decode Basis α nullifies all damage in its original form, and on its 4th instance of physical damage, 4th instance of arts damage, or when blocked, spends 2 seconds turning into an Avenger, a Special Tactician or a Phantom, and only then can it be knocked out.
- **The "Duck Lord" Strategy ('Mystery Customer')**: from round 5 on, every battle of yours and your teammates' (**Final Assault and Hidden Core included**; Unite doesn't do a separate replacement) has 0–2 ground enemies (excluding Leaders and their parts, and Bounty enemies) replaced by special versions of Duck Lord, Gopnik, Crying Thief or Fatty, appearing at the replaced enemy's time and on its route (in two-player Leader fights, each player only has the enemies heading for their own protection objective replaced); whoever knocks one out gets 1 Fund (teammates during Unite count too), and one that leaks into the protection objective costs 1 LP as usual. Duck Lord and Fatty **don't attack and can't be blocked**: Duck Lord gets +300% movement speed after taking damage; Fatty always faces the side with more of our Operators and takes −80% physical / arts damage from the front. Crying Thief is Invisible (can only be targeted while blocked), and its attacks stun the target for 3.5 seconds; Gopnik is immune to Stun, Freeze and Levitate.
- 🔍▶▶ in the top-right corner shows the enemies coming this round (preview area), and 🔍 in the top-left shows the match info (disabled Alliances, disabled Operators, Strategies).

## 5. Unite (Team)

Triggers in a round when **someone leaks enemies and someone else has a perfect battle** (wiped every enemy with no leaks): up to 2 teammates with perfect battles bring their formations (keeping their HP and skill state from the end of their battle; Operators already knocked out at the end of the battle lie in place and, per the official "forced retreat", restart a full redeploy countdown, redeploying as usual once time is up and there's enough DP — if Unite is short they may not make it back in time; start-of-battle redeploy-time reductions such as the "Draft" Improv also apply) to intercept all the enemies the other players leaked.

- Participants are chosen by the official rules: most units on the field > has active Alliances > most units still standing > earlier seat (LP doesn't matter); with two participants, the higher-ranked one (then comparing the total Stacks of active Alliances) "engages first" on the right half of the field.

- Only teammates with perfect battles can take part in Unite; **Alliances don't gain Stacks** during Unite.
- Bounty enemies killed during Unite pay Funds to the killer.
- Enemies still alive after Unite cost their original owner LP; when nobody had a perfect battle the LP is deducted directly.
- The player who leaked sees an orange "×N" tag in the middle of the top bar (next to the knockout count): the number of enemies you leaked that are still on the Unite battlefield (including those not yet spawned, **no cap**), decreasing by one each time a teammate knocks one out (when an enemy that splits or summons is knocked out, the spawned enemies count as yours too, and the number goes up accordingly); LP is simultaneously shown with min(N, 10) deducted. The leaking teammate's row in the team list on the left also shows ×N (updated in sync with the knockouts on the battlefield; hover to see whose leaked enemies they are).
- Each normal round costs each player **at most 10 LP**.

## 6. Alliances

Each Operator belongs to 1–2 Alliances. An Alliance activates once enough **distinct** members are on the field (most only count the board; "Foresight", "Marvel" and "Investor" also count the Bench).

- **Core Alliances** (8 factions: Yan, Sargon, Victoria, Kjerag, Laterano, Ægir, Siracusa, Kazimierz): activate at 3 members, with a powerful extra effect at 6.
- **Add-on Alliances** (Precision, Swift, Agile, Arcane, Durable, Aid, Foresight, Marvel, Investor, Raid, Resilient, Harmony, Assist Operator, Solo, Elite): most activate at 2 / 3 members; "Solo" needs **exactly 1** on the field, the "Elite" Alliance counts the number of Elites on the field, and Operators without a faction Alliance count as "Assist Operator".
- **Harmony**: when active, each Core Alliance that already has members on the field gets +1 to its count (just +1, no matter how many Harmony Operators there are). The Alliance details say "incl. Harmony +1", and the Harmony Operator providing that 1 (e.g. Muelsyse) is listed above the member list.
- **Yan's Protection**: with 6 or more "Yan" Operators on the field it is summoned (two with 9). It tracks the enemy with the highest aggro on the field and hovers above it; its skill "Flame of Exorcism" locks onto 1 target and **breathes fire continuously** (up to 20 seconds, dealing arts damage per second to enemies around the target and applying Burn; it doesn't normal-attack while breathing fire, and stops when the target dies or it's Silenced); when there are temporarily no enemies on the field it waits in place. It has the official "Isolated": Medics won't heal it, and allied buffs, auras and talents won't select it (enemies can still attack it).
- **Stacks**: Alliances gain Stacks through various effects, and more Stacks mean stronger effects (e.g. "Yan" ATK increases with Stacks, "Foresight" gives Funds every 10 Stacks). **Stacks persist for the whole match but only take effect while the Alliance is active**, and in most cases only active Alliances can gain Stacks. Each Alliance has at most **999 Stacks** (same as the official game, taken from the official client's constant): once at 999, further Stacks are simply discarded, and Stack-based rewards (such as "Foresight"'s Funds) stop too. Stacks gained during battle are shown **live** in the Alliance bar, and are kept after the battle until Settlement (they don't roll back during Unite either).
- **How bonuses stack**: "ATK / DEF / HP +X%" from Alliances, Strategies, Equipment, Improv cards and Attributes is **added** to a skill's "ATK +X%" and then multiplied onto the stat (official: direct multiplication), e.g. Precision +130%, Solo +60% and a +40% Equipment piece total +230%, not ×2.3×1.6×1.4. Damage multipliers like "damage increased to X%" or "damage taken +X%" (Victoria, Kjerag, Arcane, Fragile, etc.) still multiply with each other; but same-name effects count only once — when both players on one battlefield have "Arcane" active, enemies get only one layer of Arcane vulnerability, taking the higher of the two (no longer multiplied); two "Gnosis" Rigid Ice effects, or the RES reduction from two "Leonhardt" / "Texas the Omertosa", likewise count only the strongest one. "Durable"'s reflected damage is credited to the member being hit (visible in the per-player Leader damage stats).
- Each match randomly **disables** a number of Alliances (fewer in Standard Simulation), and their Operators won't appear in the shop; "Confirm Match Info" at the start lists them, and you can review them again while choosing a Strategy ("View disabled Alliances & Operators") and during the match (🔍 top-left).
- Two pieces of the same Equipment merge into a stronger piece; certain Equipment combinations can grant an Operator extra Alliance effects: when the "Damazti Isomorph" is carried together with another Alliance Equipment (such as a Victorian Hammer), the holder counts as a member of the Alliance that Equipment corresponds to. The mapping is shown in the "Talent" section of the Damazti Isomorph's detail card (Alliances disabled this match are marked; when equipped on an Operator, the active group is highlighted).

## 7. Final Assault and Hidden Core

- **Final Assault** (round 14; round 9 for Solo Standard): players are paired by seat, and each pair shares one battlefield, one defending the left half and one the right half (the right-hand player's board is mirrored left-to-right). Everyone **whittles down the same Leader HP bar together** (HP = the official Leader HP for that difficulty, taken from the current version's game data — e.g. Ultimate "OpFor: Armor" 3 600 000 — no matter how many players are alive), and you can't see the other pair's battlefield. The whole team's LP is merged into one pool, with no more 10-per-round cap. When the 120-second countdown at the top ends, the battle **continues** (no loss on timeout); from second 150 of the battle (real seconds) the whole team loses 1 LP per second. The Leader's HP reaching zero is a win (when the bar shows 0 the Leader has fallen and the battle ends immediately), and LP reaching zero is a loss — whichever hits zero first counts, and damage dealt after LP runs out no longer counts. Alliances don't gain Stacks in this phase.
- **Damage cap** (Final Assault and Hidden Core, same as the official game): **any single hit of 300000 or more on the Leader is voided entirely** — that hit deals 0 damage, doesn't count toward the Leader HP bar and shows no damage number; it isn't clamped to 300000, it counts for nothing at all (299999 works as normal). This only applies to the Leader itself (including the other Leader on a two-player battlefield); the Leader's parts, escorts, drones and other minions aren't affected, nor are normal rounds or Unite; when a part transfers damage 1:1 to the Leader, or a drone's death makes the Leader lose 2% HP, that is also checked as a single hit (currently at most 144,000, which doesn't reach the line). With extremely high Stacks the Leader can still fall within seconds (e.g. both of each player's Alliances at 999 Stacks, or all active Alliances at 999 Stacks); this comes from the official uncapped Stack-based damage multipliers (Kjerag, Arcane, Precision, etc.), and the official community also talks about "boss dies in one second".
- In the Final Assault / Hidden Core Rest Phase, deployable tiles are determined by **your half of the final battlefield** (mirrored for the right-hand player), and the tiles highlighted while dragging are where you can place: e.g. on the "Originium Flow Generator" final map, the tiles enclosed by the 1×3 orange pipes can be deployed on. Operators placed on these tiles in the Final Assault stay in place when entering the Hidden Core.
- **Leader parts**: when one of "OpFor: Armor"'s drones dies (no matter who downs it; leaking doesn't count), Armor loses 2% of the Leader HP bar's total (e.g. Ultimate 3 600 000 → 72 000 per drone; the official text doesn't specify which "max HP" this refers to, and this game uses the bar's total — an inference); every point of damage taken by the Hidden Core's "Armor Cleaver / Armor Crusher" and "Gun-Breaker Spring" is deducted 1:1 from the Leader HP bar (always counts after the Cleaver / Crusher has been knocked down; hits taken mid-dive also count — the two official texts disagree on this point, so it's an inference). The Cleaver / Crusher normally hovers next to Armor, invulnerable; when thrown it blinks to a fixed route and charges the Operator with the lowest ATK, and after being hit 15 times along the way it is 【Paralyzed】 (stunned for 10 seconds, grounded, damage taken ×1.3); after landing or when the paralysis ends, it is replaced with a new one carrying its remaining HP back at its original position. "Catastrophe" fires only 1 Armor Piercer each time (the same below 20% HP), and only hits the Operator with the highest ATK in its attack range.
- **Huge Leaders** (OpFor: Armor / Pipe, Sal Viento Bishop Quintus, Alistair, 'Samivilinn,' the Resolution of the Very North) have a hit area of about 5×3 tiles (the whole pipe block): an attack range touching any one of those tiles can hit them; they are **self-bound and can't be blocked**, and never leave their spot. Splash is judged from the enemy's center, so splash landing on nearby minions doesn't reach a huge Leader.
- **Hidden Core** (round 15, Perilous and above only): after defeating the Leader, if the team's total Stacks across active Alliances is **> 1200** (Solo > 350) and LP > 1, you enter the Hidden Core to challenge a stronger Leader. Failing it doesn't affect clearing the match.

## 8. Emotes and spectating

- "Chat" in the bottom-left opens the emote panel: 6 sets × 6 emotes; swipe left / right (or use the arrow keys) to switch sets. Emotes show as a bubble next to your avatar for 3 seconds, with a 1-second send cooldown.
- During the Rest Phase, or after your own battle ends (showing "Battle over, waiting for teammates to finish"), click a teammate's avatar on the left → "Go View" to watch their battlefield; you can't spectate while your own battle is in progress. During Unite and the Final Assault you can switch between left half / Overview / right half with ‹ ›.
- After being eliminated you can freely watch any battlefield.
- **Whoever you watch, the Alliance bar shows their Alliances**: when viewing a teammate's field or battlefield (during the Rest Phase, after the battle, or auto-spectating after elimination), the Alliance bar at the top left switches to **that teammate's Alliances, counts and Stacks**, prefixed with an amber "👁 teammate name" tag, and the Alliance details you open also say "teammate name's Alliance" (the members column only marks their Operators on the field; their Bench isn't visible); during battle the Stacks grow live (matching what they see themselves). In Unite and the Final Assault it follows ‹ ›: with the camera on the teammate's half it shows their Alliances, and on your own half it shows yours; in "Overview", if you're fighting on that battlefield it shows yours, and if you're not on it (watching Unite after leaking, spectating after elimination) it shows the teammate you chose with "Go View", or, if you never chose one, the first player on that battlefield (Unite participant no. 1 / the first player of that pair) — **never your own**. On such a battlefield, clicking a teammate's avatar → "Go View" moves the camera straight to their half, and the "👁 teammate name" on ‹ › matches the Alliance bar's tag; next to ‹ › there's "Return to Battlefield" (not shown when you're eliminated). When the Alliance bar switches person, any Alliance details opened from it close automatically. When you open the detail card of an Operator on the field, the Alliances on the card (and the Alliance details opened from it) belong to **that Operator's owner**, regardless of whom the Alliance bar is currently showing — e.g. clicking a teammate's Operator in "Overview" shows "teammate name's Alliance" with their counts and Stacks; clicking your own Operator on a teammate's half shows your own. Going back to your own field / battlefield restores your own Alliances. If you refresh the page / reconnect while watching a teammate after the battle, you return to the teammate's battlefield you were watching, with "👁 teammate name" and "Return to Battlefield" still there. Eliminated teammates no longer show Alliances (and can't be viewed).

## 9. Result titles

At Settlement titles are awarded based on performance (at most one per player, and each title goes to only one player): **Stronghold Star** (highest Leader damage, only on a win), **Steadfast Alliance** (highest total active Stacks), **Rock-Solid Defense** (least LP lost), **Elites Assemble** (most Elite promotions), **Fully Armed** (most Equipment assigned), **Big Spender** (most Funds spent).

## 10. Tips

- Early on, prioritize assembling one Alliance you can activate — it's steadier than buying scattered high-tier Operators; 3 copies of the same Operator promote automatically and give a free high-tier recruit.
- Funds don't carry over to the next round: if you haven't used them, Refresh or upgrade; when you can't afford a card you want, **Freeze** first.
- The upgrade price drops by 1 each round automatically, so if you're not in a hurry you can wait a round or two before upgrading.
- During the Rest Phase check the enemy preview at the top right; if there are flying units, keep anti-air (ranged) Operators ready; **low-altitude hovering** enemies (Skimming Sea Drifter, Lucky Flyfin) also count as aerial units, so melee Operators and Operators that only hit ground units (such as Rosmontis) can't hit them, and they only drop down after being stunned, frozen or put to sleep (a Skimming Sea Drifter that has dropped keeps crawling, and its model switches to a crawling pose).
- In Team Simulation, don't compete with teammates for the same line of Operators (the pool is shared); splitting up to build different Alliances is easier.
- When you have only about 10 LP left (at most 10 can be lost per round), prioritize securing perfect battles.
- Unite only lets teammates with perfect battles join, so a formation that reliably clears enemies is valuable for the whole team.
- Entering the Hidden Core depends on the team's **total active Stacks**: in the late game you can stack Stacks deliberately.
- Stacks cap at 999, so excess stacking is useless — move your resources to other Alliances.
- Watch out for the **damage cap** in Leader fights: any single hit of 300,000 or more is voided entirely. With Alliances at 999 Stacks, stacking on more damage multipliers (e.g. lighting up Arcane on top of 999-Stack Kjerag) can actually make your big skills deal 0 damage; teammates' debuffs such as Arcane also amplify your damage.
- **Tiles enclosed by an orange fence** (official "wall") can be deployed on, and enemies can't walk in: Operators inside attack enemies in their attack range as usual (choose the facing when placing so the attack range covers the enemies' path), but **can't block ground enemies** (enemies pushed or pulled against the fence aren't blocked by it either), and rarely get hit — most Defenders' manual skills need incoming damage to fire, so inside the fence they may never fire (Underflow's S2 is the exception, firing whenever an enemy is in attack range); Operators that only hit the single tile under their feet (such as Matterhorn) can't hit any enemy from inside.
